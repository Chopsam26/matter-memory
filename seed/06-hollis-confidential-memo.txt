SECTION 1 - PURPOSE OF THIS MEMORANDUM

Article V of the will executed today states that the omission of the client's
son, Daniel Robert Hollis, is intentional, but does not state the reasons. That
was a deliberate drafting choice: reasons stated on the face of a will become
public on allowance and can themselves supply a disappointed heir with material
for a contest.

This memorandum records those reasons contemporaneously, in the client's own
account, so that they are available to the fiduciary and to counsel if the
disposition is later challenged. It is prepared in anticipation of litigation.

SECTION 2 - THE CLIENT'S STATED REASONS

The client gave the following account in a meeting on February 28, 2022, at
which she was unaccompanied. I have summarized it in her words as closely as my
notes allow.

Section 2.1. Unrepaid advances. Between 2009 and 2018 the client and her late
husband advanced Daniel a total of approximately $180,000, across eleven
separate occasions, characterized at the time as loans. Two were documented by
promissory notes, both now past the statute of limitations. No payment of
principal or interest was ever made on any advance. The client stated that she
regards these advances as Daniel's share, "spent in advance and then some."

Section 2.2. The 2021 account incident. In August 2021 the client discovered
three transfers totaling $41,500 from her Premier Savings account to an account
she did not recognize. Daniel had been given the online banking credentials in
2019 for the limited purpose of paying her utility bills during a hospital
stay. He acknowledged making the transfers when confronted and characterized
them as a further loan. The client did not report the matter to the bank or to
law enforcement, and instructed me not to pursue recovery. The credentials were
changed on September 2, 2021.

Section 2.3. Estrangement. There has been no contact between the client and
Daniel since October 2021. The client was clear that reconciliation would not
change her decision, and asked that the instrument not be drafted to reward it.

SECTION 3 - CAPACITY AND UNDUE INFLUENCE

Section 3.1. The client was examined by Dr. Amara Osei, her primary care
physician, on March 2, 2022, at my request and with the client's consent. Dr.
Osei's letter of March 7, 2022 states that the client is oriented, understands
the nature and extent of her property, and can identify the natural objects of
her bounty. That letter is filed separately at Tab 9.

Section 3.2. Claire Hollis Bennett, the principal beneficiary, was not present
at the February 28 meeting, at the March 14 execution, or at any meeting at
which the dispositive terms were discussed. This was arranged deliberately to
remove the foundation for an undue influence claim. Both attesting witnesses
are unconnected to the family.

Section 3.3. My own assessment is that the client is of sound mind, that the
decision is long-considered rather than impulsive, and that she articulated it
consistently across three meetings spanning seven weeks.

SECTION 4 - CONTEST EXPOSURE AND STRATEGY

Section 4.1. Daniel would have standing to contest as a pretermitted heir. The
express omission language in Article V is intended to defeat that claim under
M.G.L. c. 190B, s. 2-302, which applies only to children omitted by oversight.

Section 4.2. The no-contest provision in Article V is reinforced by the fact
that Daniel takes nothing under the instruments. A forfeiture clause has no
deterrent effect against a beneficiary with nothing to forfeit. Counsel raised
with the client the option of a modest bequest - $25,000 was discussed - solely
to give the clause teeth. The client declined, stating she would not "pay him
to go away."

Section 4.3. If a contest is filed, the fiduciary should expect Sections 2.1
and 2.2 above to become central, and should be prepared to produce the bank
records for the August 2021 transfers. Those records were obtained and are
filed at Tab 11.

SECTION 5 - HANDLING

Section 5.1. This memorandum is privileged attorney work product. It is not to
be provided to any beneficiary, to Daniel R. Hollis, or to any family member,
and should not be included in any copy of the estate planning portfolio
delivered to the client or to the nominated fiduciary.

Section 5.2. In the event of a contest, release of this memorandum should be
evaluated against the possibility that disclosure waives privilege over the
surrounding file.

Section 5.3. Review this memorandum at each three-year plan review. If the
client reconciles with Daniel and wishes to revise the disposition, a new
instrument is required; a codicil should not be used, as it would draw
attention to the change.
